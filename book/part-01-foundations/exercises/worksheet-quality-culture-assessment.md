## Purpose

Help a delivery team discuss the behaviours and conditions that enable quality. The worksheet is a qualitative reflection and improvement tool; it is not a maturity model, performance rating, certification, or organisational score.

## QE Capability Developed

Collaboration analysis, evidence-based improvement planning, quality-culture facilitation, and constructive risk communication.

## Prerequisites

- Read Chapter 7 and review the [Quality Culture Flywheel](../../../diagrams/chapter-07-quality-culture-flywheel.md).
- Use an illustrative scenario, a safely anonymised team context, or the Northstar Claims scenario in Chapter 7.

## Expected Outputs

- qualitative culture evidence across ten areas;
- observed strengths and risks;
- three or fewer prioritised improvements; and
- a 90-day experiment plan with evidence and review points.

## Portfolio Relevance

This is usually an internal team-learning artefact rather than a public portfolio item. A learner may create a fictional or strongly anonymised facilitation example to demonstrate Quality Engineering leadership. Do not publish colleague feedback, incident details, internal metrics, or employer-specific culture assessments.

## Reflection Questions

1. Which behaviour most slows the discovery or use of quality evidence?
2. Which metric or incentive could reward activity while harming the customer outcome?
3. Where is accountability unclear, and where is it mistakenly concentrated in one role?
4. What evidence would show that an improvement changed team behaviour rather than only meeting attendance?

## Completion Criteria

- [ ] Every qualitative assessment includes evidence rather than a score alone.
- [ ] At least one strength and one risk are identified.
- [ ] The improvement plan has no more than three actions.
- [ ] Each action has an owner, a review point, and evidence of progress.
- [ ] The assessment is not presented as a maturity rating or certification result.
